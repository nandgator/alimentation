# Guides

Alimentation keeps the accounts of a small kitchen that feeds a few people every weekday: **who ate, what each meal cost, who paid, and what is still owed.** These guides are for the people who use it. They are short, they take one job at a time, and they use no jargon.

## The idea in one minute

- **Everyone gets the day's meal unless they say otherwise.** Nobody logs a plain lunch. People log only the exceptions: a day on leave, a switched dish, two extra chapatis.
- **The bill is worked out from the rules, never typed in.** Prices, the weekly menu, holidays and a few kitchen rules go in once. The month's bill follows.
- **Paying takes two people.** A member says they paid. The supplier confirms it. Once decided, that decision cannot be quietly changed.
- **Everything has a name and a time on it.** Who did what, and when, is written down, and the people a change affects can read it.
- **People are given room.** Someone who was away, or a supplier who was unavailable, can catch up until the month is closed. Late is allowed. Quiet is not.

## What it is not

It does not move money. People pay each other by UPI, cash or bank transfer, and Alimentation keeps the record and the agreement. It is not an accounting package, and it does not send reminders. It is a careful ledger that a few people who trust each other can all read.
