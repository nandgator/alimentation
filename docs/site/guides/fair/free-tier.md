# The free tier and what it counts

A small kitchen runs free on shared hosting. The numbers below are the defaults of the shared service. Whoever runs an instance can change them, and a dedicated instance has no limits at all.

| Limit             | Free tier                                     |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **People**        | 25                                            |
| **Entries**       | 5,000                                         |
| **Actions a day** | 1,000, all together, across the whole kitchen |
| **Storage**       | 20 MB                                         |

## What an "entry" is

An entry is a **thing someone wrote down**: one logged exception, one payment claim, one supplier decision, one settlement. That is all.

The ordinary meals are **not** entries. The default meal is worked out when it is needed, so a member who eats every weekday and never says otherwise uses **none**.

The 5,000 is **one pool for the whole kitchen**, not per person, and it is not "5,000 across 365 days across everyone". A kitchen of 25 people who each log three exceptions, make one claim, and have it decided by the supplier, every month, writes 25 × 5 × 12 = **1,500** entries a year. That uses a third of the allowance a year, which is three years of headroom.

## What an "action" is

An action is one request the app makes to the server. Opening a screen makes a few; adding an entry makes one. The 1,000 a day are shared by everyone in the kitchen.

## The meter

The admin sees the meter in **Your data**. It warns at **70 percent** and again at **90 percent**, well before anything stops, and shows each limit on its own.

## At the limit

At 100 percent of any limit you have **14 days**. After that, **new writes are refused** with a message that says why. **Reading and taking a copy are never blocked**, at any time, even over the limit.

You can leave the free tier with one action: [move to a place of your own](/guides/fair/your-data/), with nothing lost.
