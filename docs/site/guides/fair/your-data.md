# Copying, moving and deleting

All three are in **Your data**, which only the admin can open. Your data is yours to take at any time.

## Take a copy

**Download everything** gives you one file, named after your kitchen's address and ending `.alimentation.json`. It holds every record in the kitchen, in a form other programs can read, with a seal that shows if anything was changed after it was made. It works at any time, including when the kitchen is over its limits.

The file contains everyone's names and every payment. Keep it as carefully as you would a paper ledger.

## Move to a place of your own

When a kitchen outgrows the free tier, or you would rather hold the data yourself, **move** it. There are two destinations:

- **A database of my own.** Someone technical, a friend or whoever looks after your computers, sets up an empty database and gives you its address.
- **An Alimentation of my own.** Another copy of Alimentation, running on a computer of yours. Its owner gives you its web address and a secret code that lets your kitchen be sent there.

How a move goes:

1. **Check it first.** The app does a dry run and tells you whether it will work. Nothing changes.
2. **Confirm** by typing the kitchen's address.
3. **The copy is made and checked to the paisa.** Every statement is recomputed on the other side and compared.
4. **The switch.** From then on the kitchen answers from the new place. Everyone signs in as before, with the same email, and is matched to the same person automatically.
5. **This copy stays read-only for seven days**, in case you change your mind, then is removed.

If anything cannot be carried across exactly, the move is **refused**. Your rules are never approximated.

The technical side of a move, for whoever sets up the other end, is in [Bundles and moves](/docs/lifecycle/bundle-and-move/).

> [!NOTE]
> A move never leaves two live copies. If the other side may have accepted the kitchen and the answer was lost, this one stays read-only and waits for a person, rather than risk two.

## Delete everything

**Delete everything** erases the kitchen:

1. **The keys are destroyed first.** From that moment nothing in the kitchen can be read by anyone, including whoever runs the service.
2. **The database is removed.**
3. **Every record of who was in it is removed.**

Then you get a **receipt**. It records that the keys were destroyed, the database removed and the directory entry gone, and **names no one**: no kitchen name, no address, no person.

You confirm by typing the kitchen's address. It cannot be undone. Take a copy first if you may want the records: copies you have already downloaded are yours and are not touched.
